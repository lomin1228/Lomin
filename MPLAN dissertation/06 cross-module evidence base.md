## 3. THE ECONOMIC MECHANISM — why publicness gets squeezed (BPLN0041)

Your viability material supplies the *causal engine* linking neoliberalisation to degraded public space. This is what turns a description into an explanation (Distinction-level).

- **Residual logic:** `RLV = GDV − build costs − fees − finance − developer profit − planning obligations`, then compare to **Benchmark Land Value (BLV = EUV + 10–20% premium, or AUV)**. Because **developer profit (PPG: 15–20% of GDV) and BLV are treated as fixed prior claims**, affordable housing, public realm and social infrastructure become the **adjustable residual** — the first things cut.
- **The "circularity" critique (RICS, 2021 — best quote):** *"Higher land prices reduce developer contributions and reduced developer contribution expectations can fuel higher land prices."*
- **Depoliticisation / calculative practices:** developers hold the modelling expertise; LPAs are "critical friend". → Foye (2022) on depoliticisation; McAllister, Street & Wyatt (2016) "governing calculative practices".
- **Land value capture & the unearned increment** (Churchill epigraph; Harvey): public investment lifts land value, captured privately → ties straight to your neoliberalisation frame.
- **Quantitative artefact you already have:** the **government Development Appraisal Tool (DAT)** Excel models in `bpln0041/FW_ Economics of viability/` (a real site + sensitivity variants for GDV/profit/sales-rate). → run these to *show numerically* how delivery model + obligations move RLV and thus the deliverable quantity/quality of public space. **This is a ready-made quantitative chapter.**

## 4. QUANTITATIVE VALUATION METHODS for "quality of public space" (BPLN0041 → Green Book 2026)

Easy-to-interpret, defensible, examiner-checkable methods — these complement the publicness index:

- **Hedonic pricing (your headline green-space method).** Green Book 2026 §8.14 verbatim: estimate the value of green space by how *"average house prices vary with the amount of green space in different areas."* → quantify the value of quality public space. Cite **Gibbons, Mourato & Resende (2014)** 'The amenity value of English nature' and **Ahlfeldt & Holman (2018)** for *design/architectural* amenity.
- **Travel cost; stated preference / contingent valuation; WELLBY wellbeing valuation; natural capital** — the Green Book hierarchy (market → revealed → stated → wellbeing).
- **Summary metrics:** `NPV/NPSV = PVB − PVC`; `BCR = PVB / PVC`. Caveat to cite: a BCR < 1 can still be value-for-money where benefits are unmonetisable (i.e. publicness is partly unmonetisable — a useful limitation to flag).
