That is a documented decision *not* to adopt LVT, with reasons attached. W1's terms of reference explicitly include "links to the planning system" — so if R5 scores high anywhere, it is here.

**Why Scotland matters:** the Land Commission explicitly **declined** to recommend a single LVT, arguing instead for bringing all land onto the valuation roll and building a cadastre first. Predicted R4/R5 scores are the highest in your corpus, and Scotland has land tax powers it has chosen not to use in that form. Note that Scotland still uses **1991** council tax values, like England.

## Jurisdiction 4 — Northern Ireland (the control case)

Domestic rates on **2005 capital values**, with a cap treating anything above £400,000 as though worth £400,000 (Mirrlees *et al.*, 2011, ch. 16).

Northern Ireland is the natural experiment sitting inside the UK that everyone ignores: it abolished banding, uses a proportional capital-value base, and has revalued this century. Two to three pages, drawn from Department of Finance NI and Land & Property Services documents. **Do not expand this into a full case** — it is a control, and the temptation to grow it will eat your word count.

## International benchmarks (desk only, 3 pages combined, Chapter 5)

Purpose: **transition design**, nothing else. Resist writing a comparative chapter.

- **Denmark (2024)** — full reassessment on 2022 values, rates cut to hold liabilities flat, collection nationalised, and a permanent non-transferable discount for existing owners that resets on sale. The transition problem solved, and a lock-in problem created.
- **ACT, Australia (2012–)** — 20-year revenue-neutral transition from stamp duty to broad-based land taxation via general rates. Two independent 2020 evaluations; effects still hard to isolate after eight years.
- **Pennsylvania** — split-rate as the only long-run evidence base on urban form (Oates and Schwab, 1997; Banzhaf and Lavery, 2010).

## Coding protocol

**Unit of analysis:** the document. Roughly 25–30 documents total across four jurisdictions — comfortably enough for a 15,000-word study and comparable in scale to the 12-document minimum in your Thamesmead proposal.

**Instrument:** Rationale Coding Frame, R1–R7, scored 0–3 (see `00 pivot brief`, §5).

**Register fields:** document ID · jurisdiction · issuing body · date · type (legislation / consultation / response / commissioned research / evaluation / parliamentary) · length · R1–R7 scores · supporting quotation for each score ≥ 2 · coder notes.

**Reliability.** You are a single coder, which is a real limitation — state it. Mitigate three ways: (a) publish the full rubric with anchored descriptors in the appendix; (b) **double-code a 20% sample two weeks apart and report intra-rater agreement** — cheap, quantitative, and examiners notice; (c) quote the evidence for every score of 2 or 3 so a reader can audit you.

**Provenance rule (write this into the methodology).** Admissible: legislation, government publications, commissioned research, official statistics, Hansard, published consultation responses, on-record speeches with a verifiable transcript. Inadmissible: unattributed press reporting, anonymous briefing, social media, and any "sources say" claim about intentions.

## Practical notes

- **Everything in this corpus is free.** No archive visits, no FOI, no paywalls, no interviews. Ethics remains low-risk desk research — identical to the approved position in your submitted proposal.
- **Download and date-stamp everything immediately.** Consultation pages get taken down and replaced by response documents. Save PDFs locally with retrieval dates; you will need those for Harvard references to online government sources anyway.
- **Word-count discipline.** The failure mode for this dissertation is the history chapter eating the analysis. Betterment history 1909–2026 gets 800 words in Chapter 2, and a timeline table in the appendix. That is all.
- **The falsification test — do this in week one.** Read E2 and W4 back to back. If the English consultation turns out to discuss land use behaviour seriously, your premise is wrong and you need to know now. Two afternoons, and it either de-risks the whole project or saves you three months.
