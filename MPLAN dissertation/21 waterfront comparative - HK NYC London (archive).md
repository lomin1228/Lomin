## Comparison framework

### Design principles (common coding lens)
- Access and publicness (continuous waterfront, permeability)
- Urban form and edge conditions
- Landscape and ecology
- Mobility and connectivity
- Identity and heritage
- Programming and economics

### Policy and delivery instruments
- Statutory status (plan vs guidance)
- Design control tools (codes, manuals, lease parameters)
- Land/lease mechanisms and obligations
- Delivery governance (quasi-public authorities, partnerships)
- Funding model (self-financing, capture, public capital)

## City summaries (from Mar 2026 analysis)

### Hong Kong — Kai Tak

**Design principles:** Parameter-based design control via lease conditions; 11 km promenade with spatial/connectivity/sustainability/character values (Harbourfront Commission, 2018).

**Instruments:** Land administration + lease conditions; promenade design-control toolkit for mixed public/private deliverers.

**Strengths:** High implementation capacity; legible public-realm identity; operational clarity for mixed deliverers.

**Constraints:** Publicness may remain quasi-private; sustainability framed as flexibility not climate adaptation; harbourfront politics structurally contested (Ng, 2006; Cheung and Tang, 2015).

### New York — Hudson River Park

**Design principles:** Public access, environmental education, habitat enhancement as core mission (Hudson River Park Trust, 2021).

**Instruments:** Statutory park + estuarine sanctuary; quasi-public Trust with self-financing imperative (New York State, 1998; Fox, 2024).

**Strengths:** Resilience-by-governance; ecology formalised not mitigated; clear legitimacy narrative.

**Constraints:** Fiscal structure steers toward concessions/eventisation; ESMP focuses on water section not adjacent development controls.

### London — Royal Docks

**Design principles:** Good Growth framing; place-based regeneration; public realm coherence via design toolbox (GLA, 2023; Royal Docks Team, n.d.).

**Instruments:** OAPF as strategic guidance; supplementary design guides; enterprise zone delivery context (GLA, 2018).

**Strengths:** Multi-objective integration; implementation usability; explicit resilience/connectivity agenda.

**Constraints:** Guidance vs enforceability; public benefit distribution tensions; dock-edge activation trade-offs.

## Cross-case synthesis (design philosophy → instrument → outcome)

| City | Where design philosophy lives | Strength/constraint pattern |
|---|---|---|
| HK | Embedded in development control (parameters/leases) | Strong design fidelity; weak inclusive governance |
| NYC | Institutional and ecological (Trust + statutory mandate) | Strong ecological continuity; fiscal pressure on edge uses |
| London | Strategic + toolkit-based (OAPF + design guides) | Strong coordination; weaker enforceability |

**Comparative proposition:** Waterfront outcomes are mediated by **the instrument that carries the design principle** — lease control (HK), statutory management (NYC), strategic framework (London).
