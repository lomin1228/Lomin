## Diagnosis: what went wrong

These are **writing and design clarity** problems, not a rejection of the topic. The underlying idea — document-led affordability semantics on paired London sites — is sound and aligns with Ben's June guidance. The proposal buried the argument under:

1. **Undefined terms** — "drift", "regulatory regime", "Model A/B" used before defined
2. **Overloaded RQ** — one sentence trying to do semantics + deliverability + replacement + two case pairs + interviews
3. **Methods without justification** — ASI described but not anchored in document-analysis methodology literature
4. **Interview scope creep** — added late without recruitment plan, sample logic, or analysis method
5. **Case selection by convenience** — same-site pairs are strong, but the proposal did not cite Yin/Flyvbjerg on *why* paired comparison fits this question

## Fix list (if staying on Path A)

### 1. Statement of context — rewrite in four moves

| Move | Content | ~words |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Contract** | Post-war London: "affordable" = social rent at scale (Thamesmead 1968; Heygate) | 80 |
| **2. Instrument shift** | From 1980s: obligations moved to **Section 106** — explain in one sentence: private developers negotiate affordable housing as a planning condition in exchange for permission | 60 |
| **3. Semantic expansion** | "Affordable" now includes affordable rent (≤80% market), shared ownership, commuted sums — label decoupled from distributional effect (Scanlon, 2017) | 60 |
| **4. Testable gap** | Two same-site regenerations under **different instruments** (viability-era S106 vs programme-led new towns) let you compare what documents *commit to* — that is the drift | 80 |

**Define "regulatory regime" once:** Era 1 = direct public provision (pre-1980s); Era 2 = residual viability + S106 (2000s–2020s); Era 3 = programme-led targets (new towns 2025–).

### 2. Research question — one sentence

> **How do published planning documents on Thamesmead and Elephant Park define, commit to, and deliver "affordable housing" under contrasting regulatory instruments — and does the meaning of the label change systematically between them?**

### 3. Sub-questions — five, max two lines each

1. What tenure models did Phase 1 Thamesmead and the Heygate baseline embody in primary documents?
2. What affordable housing obligations do contemporary consent packages at each site record?
3. How do replacement and displacement provisions differ between viability-led clearance (Elephant Park) and phased whole-place regeneration (Thamesmead)?
4. How does the Affordability Semantics Index (ASI) vary across coded document phases?
5. Do London-wide rent-to-income trends (1990–2025) support the documentary findings?

### 4. Objectives — bullet points only

- Harvest and register planning, programme, and developer documents for both case pairs
- Code ASI scores and replacement/displacement provisions using a pre-registered rubric
- Compare within pairs (old vs new) and across pairs (Thamesmead vs Elephant Park)
- Contextualise findings with a supporting rent-to-income series (ONS/GLA)
- Derive document-grounded recommendations — no speculative viability model

### 5. Case selection — add one paragraph

Cite **Yin (2018)** on explanatory case study design: paired same-site comparison controls for locational variables while isolating institutional change. Cite **Flyvbjerg (2006)** on case selection for maximum information — Thamesmead and Heygate are "most likely" cases where affordability semantics shifted visibly because both involve demolition/rebuild on post-war estates with published document trails. Justify *why two pairs, not six*: 15,000-word limit; depth over breadth; Ben's June steer toward document feasibility.

### 6. Document coding — justify with method literature

- **Bowen (2009)** — document analysis as a systematic method for policy research
- **Bryman (2016)** — content analysis; coding reliability
- Your ASI is a **directed content analysis** (Hsieh and Shannon, 2005): categories derived from literature (Scanlon; Preece et al.) then applied to documents
- State you will **pre-register the rubric** and report inter-coder check if feasible (even self-check after 2-week gap)

### 7. Interviews — pick one path

**Option A (recommended — matches Ben's June steer):** Drop interviews. Proposal becomes document-only. Add one sentence: "Published documents are the evidential core; local perspectives are noted from secondary sources (Lees and Ferreri, 2016) but not primary interview data."

**Option B (if Ben insists on qualitative depth):** Reduce to **4–6 interviews maximum**, one stakeholder type, one case only (e.g. Peabody regeneration team or Thamesmead community organisation). Specify: purposive sampling via document contacts; semi-structured; thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006); ethics via UCL REC if needed.

Do **not** keep "10 interviews across two cases" — the marker is right that this spreads too thin.
